## How I work

### Read the rules of an unfamiliar field first

I once called myself **Metamon** (Ditto).  
It used to mean "learns anything fast"; now it means "reads the rules of an unfamiliar place and reshapes itself to fit."

When I joined Alpha Weather, meteorology was new to me.  
I learned the vocabulary, had NIMS researchers and staff walk me through the workflow, and built the technical direction on that understanding.

### Play it through before building

I tend to think of exceptions before the happy path.  
What if the user does the opposite? Cancels halfway? Leaves a value empty?  
So most questions during implementation are cases I have already walked through.

When we showed Arcanum Nights at PlayX4, I watched expressions, click speed, and repeated actions, not just clears, and wrote them up as a QA document.

### Translate between disciplines

On the same game, developers look at structure, artists at expression, planners at flow.  
Having coded, drawn, and made music myself, I can put each side's terms into the others' language.

Early on I took on everything I could.  
When work piled up on one person, the team slowed down; since then I spend my time dividing work and connecting perspectives.

### Complex behind, simple in front

I used to think more features meant a better product.  
Now I think good planning considers every case behind the scenes and shows only what is needed up front.

## Interests

I am drawn to math, music, art, and games, and most of my ideas come from connecting them.  
My favorite moment is when someone tries what I made and says, "Oh, this is nice."
